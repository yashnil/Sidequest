# Independent travel-quality requirements: two new scenarios

These requirements were written from public sources without looking at any generated itinerary or at Sidequest code. They describe what a competent human travel advisor would expect a plan to contain. They follow the format of `eval-v2/requirements.md`.

**How to read this**
- Every claim cites a URL. Several official sites could not be fetched: nts.org.uk returned 403, and the VisitScotland, Highland Council and Eilean Donan pages either returned 404 or did not answer. Where a claim rests only on an operator, blog or news page, it is marked *(secondary)*. Where it rests on general advisor knowledge that was not checked against a source in this pass, it is marked *unverified*.
- **Sun times.** timeanddate.com refuses automated fetches, so sunrise and sunset were computed with the NOAA sunrise/sunset algorithm (zenith 90.833°). They should agree with a calculator to within about ±2 minutes. Local offsets are 2027 summer time: Scotland is UTC+1 (BST); France, Italy and Switzerland are UTC+2 (CEST). The method is described at https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/calcdetails.html. These are horizon times. In deep Alpine valleys the sun leaves the trail earlier than the computed sunset.
- "Daily limit" means time spent moving between places (driving, transfers, buses, lifts). It does not include walking that is itself the activity. For the trek, walking limits are given separately.

## 11. mountain-road-trip: Scottish Highlands road trip, 6–13 June 2027, couple, rental car

**What the request means.** The couple want a **self-drive tour of the West Highlands and islands**: glens, sea lochs, mountain roads, castles and short walks. There are 7 nights. The trip should cover the signature landscapes without becoming a marathon drive.

**Defining experiences (any good answer must have them)**
- **Glen Coe**: the A82 through the glen, the Three Sisters viewpoint beside the road, and the National Trust for Scotland visitor centre. The NTS has cared for Glencoe since 1935. Short woodland walks start at the visitor centre, and the Hidden Valley (Coire Gabhail) walk takes about 2.5–3 hours return on rough ground. https://www.nts.org.uk/visit/places/glencoe/highlights/outdoor-adventures (seen in search results; nts.org.uk refused direct fetches) and https://outaboutscotland.com/glencoe-visitor-centre *(secondary)*
- **The Isle of Skye**: the Trotternish loop (Old Man of Storr, Quiraing, Kilt Rock), the Fairy Pools in Glen Brittle, Neist Point and Portree. You reach Skye either by bridge at Kyle of Lochalsh or by CalMac ferry from Mallaig to Armadale, a crossing of about 45 minutes. https://www.calmac.co.uk/en-gb/destinations/skye/ ; attraction list *(secondary)*: https://www.earthtrekkers.com/isle-of-skye-best-things-to-do/
- **Glenfinnan and the Road to the Isles** (A830, Fort William to Mallaig). This road passes the Glenfinnan Viaduct, which the Jacobite steam train crosses. West Coast Railways lists 2027 Jacobite departures from 5 April to 29 October, with bookings "coming soon". https://westcoastrailways.co.uk
- **Eilean Donan Castle** at Dornie, near Kyle of Lochalsh, on the road to Skye. https://www.eileandonancastle.com/
- **At least one great Highland drive**: Glen Coe and Rannoch Moor, the Road to the Isles, Wester Ross/Torridon, or the Bealach na Bà to Applecross. The Bealach na Bà has gradients of about 20% and hairpins, and is signed as unsuitable for learner drivers, very large vehicles and caravans. https://outaboutscotland.com/bealach-na-ba-highlands/ *(secondary)*
- **Optional**: Loch Ness and Urquhart Castle near Drumnadrochit. Tickets are sold for a timed entry, and pre-booking guarantees entry. https://www.historicenvironment.scot/visit-a-place/places/urquhart-castle/ Other options are the Cairngorms, and Ben Nevis or the Nevis Range gondola from Fort William. *unverified*

**Recommended structure**
- 7 nights in 3, at most 4, bases, each for 2–3 nights:
  - Glencoe or Fort William (2 nights)
  - Skye, with Portree or the Trotternish side a good base (3 nights)
  - Wester Ross/Torridon or Inverness/Loch Ness (2 nights)

  Fly-in gateways are Glasgow, Edinburgh or Inverness. *unverified (advisor norm)*
- A natural one-way flow runs from Glasgow via Loch Lomond to Glen Coe, Fort William, the Road to the Isles and the Mallaig–Armadale ferry to Skye. It leaves Skye by the bridge and Eilean Donan, continues to Torridon/Applecross or Loch Ness, and ends at Inverness. The reverse direction also works. A one-way rental with a drop-off fee, or a loop back to Glasgow, are both acceptable.
- **Movement is a rental car.** Many Highland and Skye roads are **single-track with passing places**. Use a passing place to let oncoming cars pass and to let following cars overtake, and never park in one. https://scotland.police.uk/what-s-happening/news/2021/july/advice-for-people-heading-to-the-north-of-scotland-this-summer ; https://www.undiscoveredscotland.co.uk/usfeatures/singletrack/ *(secondary)*
- **Approximate drive times** *unverified*:
  - Glasgow to Glencoe: about 2 h 15 min
  - Glencoe to Fort William: about 30 min
  - Fort William to Mallaig: about 1 h 15 min
  - Fort William to Portree via Kyle: about 2 h 45 min
  - Portree to Inverness: about 2 h 45 min
  - Portree to Neist Point: about 1 h each way
  - The Trotternish loop: about 1.5–2 h of driving before stops
- **The North Coast 500 does not fit.** It is "just over 500 miles (516 to be exact)", and its own featured itineraries run 7–9 days. It suggests "a few days exploring one section" for shorter trips. https://www.northcoast500.com/ With Glen Coe and Skye as well, the full NC500 cannot fit in 7 nights.

**Hard requirements**
- Includes **Glen Coe** and **Skye**. A Highlands road trip without either is a weak answer.
- Book the **Mallaig–Armadale car ferry** in advance if the plan uses it. CalMac "strongly advise booking in advance". https://www.calmac.co.uk/en-gb/destinations/skye/
- Book **Skye accommodation** well ahead. Skye is small and heavily visited in summer, and islanders have asked for help with overcrowding. *(secondary)* https://www.inkl.com/news/skye-islanders-call-for-help-with-overcrowding-after-tourism-surge
- Plan **honest drive times on single-track roads**. Average speeds are well below the 60 mph legal limit. *(secondary)* https://www.undiscoveredscotland.co.uk/usfeatures/singletrack/
- Account for **parking pressure** at the Storr, Fairy Pools and Quiraing by going early or late. Guides report that the Storr car park fills by about 09:00 in summer. *(secondary)* https://www.livebreathescotland.com/things-to-do-on-the-isle-of-skye/
- If the Jacobite steam train is included, say it must be pre-booked and that 2027 dates and fares are not yet published. https://westcoastrailways.co.uk

**Red flags**
- **The full NC500**, or most of it, squeezed into this trip alongside Skye and Glen Coe.
- **Repeated days of more than about 4–5 hours' driving.** Ten-hour driving days are a clear flag.
- **No Glen Coe or no Skye.**
- **Impossible geography**: driving to Mull, Harris/Lewis or Orkney with no ferry; treating a sea loch as crossable by road where it is not; or a "day trip to Skye" from Edinburgh or Glasgow. That is about 5 hours each way. *unverified*
- **Single-track time ignored.** Examples are budgeting the Applecross peninsula or Glen Brittle at main-road speeds, or planning a campervan or caravan over the Bealach na Bà.
- **Sunset framed at the wrong hour.** A "sunset dinner at 19:00/20:00" is wrong when sunset is about 22:10–22:20.
- **A midge-blind plan.** Long dusk picnics or wild camping by still water in the west with no repellent or midge net mentioned.
- **Edinburgh- or city-heavy days.** City sightseeing taking more than about one day of a 7-night Highlands request.
- **Wild camping or overnighting in a passing place or lay-by.** *unverified*

**Timing facts (6–13 June 2027)**
- **Sun times** (computed; check at https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/uk/fort-william and https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/uk/portree):

  | Place and date | Sunrise | Sunset |
  |---|---|---|
  | Fort William, 6 Jun | 04:32 | 22:07 |
  | Fort William, 9 Jun | 04:30 | 22:10 |
  | Fort William, 13 Jun | 04:28 | 22:14 |
  | Portree, 6 Jun | 04:32 | 22:16 |
  | Portree, 9 Jun | 04:29 | 22:19 |
  | Portree, 13 Jun | 04:27 | 22:23 |
  | Inverness, 6 Jun | 04:23 | 22:09 |
  | Inverness, 13 Jun | 04:19 | 22:16 |

  Civil twilight lasts until about midnight, and there is no true darkness near the solstice. *unverified*
- **Midges.** The season runs roughly from late May or June to early September, worst in July and August and in the damp west Highlands and islands. Trap counts traditionally show a first peak in early June. https://www.netweather.tv/weather-forecasts/news/8876-midges-the-highlands-sunshine-and-spray-how-the-weather-affects-the-season *(secondary)* ; https://www.highlandexperience.com/blog/scotlands-midge-season-explained/ *(secondary)*. Repellent and a head net should be on the packing list.
- **Weather.** Fort William in June has highs of about 15–18 °C, lows of about 11 °C and about 77 mm of rain a month, and conditions change quickly. *(secondary, third-party climate sites)* https://www.weerplaza.nl/wereldweer/europa/groot-brittannie/fort-william/2648354/klimaat/temperatuur ; https://www.ou-et-quand.net/partir/quand/europe/ecosse/fort-william/. June is often one of the drier, brighter months in the west. *unverified*
- **Crowds.** These dates are shoulder-to-high season. Scottish school holidays begin in late June, so the dates fall before them. *unverified* Skye honeypots are busy every day in summer. *(secondary, see above)*

**Reasonable daily limits**
- About **3–4 hours of driving a day** typical, with at most 5 hours on a single repositioning day. Single-track roads, photo stops and ferries make 200–250 km a full day. *unverified (advisor norm)*
- No more than about one repositioning day per base.

## 12. point-to-point-trek: Tour du Mont Blanc trek, 10–19 July 2027, three friends, walking hut to hut / village to village

**What the request means.** Three friends want to **walk the Tour du Mont Blanc (TMB)**: a circuit around the Mont Blanc massif through France, Italy and Switzerland, moving every night between refuges, gîtes and village hotels. There are 10 days. The trip is the walk itself, not sightseeing from a base.

**Defining experiences**
- **Walking the TMB as a continuous point-to-point route** of about 165–170 km with about 9,000–10,000 m of ascent, normally anticlockwise. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_du_Mont_Blanc *(secondary)* ; the official site gives "170 km", about 10,000 m and "10 days": https://www.autourdumontblanc.com/en/
- **All three countries**:
  - France: Les Houches, Les Contamines, Les Chapieux, Chamonix valley
  - Italy: Val Veny, Courmayeur, Val Ferret
  - Switzerland: La Fouly, Champex-Lac, Trient, Col de la Forclaz

  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_du_Mont_Blanc
- **The big cols**:
  - Col de la Croix du Bonhomme, 2,479 m
  - Col de la Seigne, 2,516 m (the France–Italy border)
  - Grand Col Ferret, 2,537 m (Italy–Switzerland)
  - Col de Balme, 2,191 m (Switzerland–France)

  The highest points are the optional Col des Fours and Fenêtre d'Arpette, both 2,665 m. https://en.chamonix.com/objet_touristique/6318 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_du_Mont_Blanc
- **Refuge nights on half board**. Examples are Rifugio Elisabetta, Rifugio Bonatti, Refuge des Mottets and Refuge de la Flégère. Half board is the standard arrangement, and walkers bring a sleeping-bag liner. https://www2.montourdumontblanc.com/en/actualites/when-refuge-bookings-open-for-the-season
- **The Grand Balcon views of the Mont Blanc massif** from the Italian Val Ferret balcony (Bonatti) and from the Aiguilles Rouges side (Flégère, Lac Blanc). *unverified (well-known)*

**Recommended structure**
- **Standard 11 stages**, anticlockwise from Les Houches. Figures vary by source and variant:

  | Stage | From → to | km | Ascent / descent (m) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | Les Houches → Les Contamines | 16 | 646 / 633 |
  | 2 | Les Contamines → Les Chapieux | 18 | 1,316 / 929 |
  | 3 | Les Chapieux → Rifugio Elisabetta | 15 | 1,004 / 258 |
  | 4 | Rifugio Elisabetta → Courmayeur | 18 | 460 / 1,560 |
  | 5 | Courmayeur → Rifugio Bonatti | 12 | 860 / 101 |
  | 6 | Rifugio Bonatti → La Fouly | 20 | 895 / 1,410 |
  | 7 | La Fouly → Champex | 15 | 420 / 565 |
  | 8 | Champex → Col de la Forclaz | 16 | 742 / 682 |
  | 9 | Col de la Forclaz → Tré-le-Champ | 13 | 1,069 / 1,178 |
  | 10 | Tré-le-Champ → Refuge de la Flégère | 8 | 733 / 257 |
  | 11 | Refuge de la Flégère → Les Houches | 17 | 772 / 1,546 |

  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_du_Mont_Blanc *(secondary; the leg table totals 168 km / 8,917 m)*. The Chamonix tourist office's 6-day hotel version gives Les Houches → Les Contamines as 12 km and 700/1,300 m. https://en.chamonix.com/objet_touristique/6318
- **Fitting it into 10 days**: walk 9–10 stages, using public transport or lifts to shorten one or two. https://alpinehikers.com/public-transport-on-the-tmb *(secondary)* The usual compressions are:
  - the **Bellevue cable car** from Les Houches (https://en.chamonix.com/objet_touristique/6318)
  - the **Courmayeur local bus** up Val Ferret to Arnouvaz/Arnuva. In 2026 it was free from 11 June, with the Val Ferret timetable from 27 June. *(secondary)* https://alpinehikers.com/public-transport-on-the-tmb
  - the **TMR "Tour du Mont-Blanc" bus** from Ferret/La Fouly to Champex-Lac via Orsières, which runs daily to 27 September. https://www.tmrsa.ch/en/tmb/ ; https://www.valais.ch/en/shop/transport/tour-du-mont-blanc-bus-service-between-ferret-and-champex-lac
  - the **Chamonix bus** between Les Houches, Chamonix, Les Praz/Flégère and Le Tour. https://en.chamonix.com/objet_touristique/6318 mentions the Le Tour–Chamonix bus. The Chamonix bus guide: https://www.chamonix.com/sites/default/files/media/brochures/Guide-Printemps-Bus-2026.pdf
  - the **Courmayeur–Chamonix coach** through the Mont Blanc tunnel, about 35 minutes. *(secondary)* https://www.flixbus.fr/voyages-car/courmayeur-chamonix-mont-blanc
- **Gateway**: Geneva airport, then a transfer of about 1–1.5 hours to Chamonix or Les Houches. Start and finish at Les Houches or Chamonix. *unverified*
- **Luggage transfer** is offered between valley hotels by commercial services; Mountain Drop-Offs works with Transport Besson. Typical rules are one soft bag of up to 15 kg each, sent out by about 08:00 and arriving in the late afternoon. Refuges high on the route are not served, so on refuge nights you carry what you need. https://www.mountaindropoffs.com/tour-du-mont-blanc/tdmb-bag-transfer/ *(secondary)* ; https://orbisways.com/en/complet-tour-du-mont-blanc-in-comfort-extended *(secondary)*. Whether a given high refuge is unreachable by road is *unverified*.
- **Movement is on foot**, helped by buses and lifts. No car is needed, and a car is a liability on a one-way loop.

**Hard requirements**
- **Book accommodation months ahead.** Bookings for summer 2027 opened on **15 October 2026** for every refuge and gîte in the TMB Wardens' Association. Mid-July to mid-August "requires especially early planning", and walkers should book the most in-demand stages first. https://www2.montourdumontblanc.com/en/actualites/when-refuge-bookings-open-for-the-season ; https://www2.montourdumontblanc.com/en/actualites/booking-a-refuge-the-complete-guide. Three people need three beds every night, which is harder than one.
- **The Refuge de la Croix du Bonhomme is closed for all of summer 2027.** It closed on 17 August 2026 for renovation and reopens in spring 2029. https://ffcam.fr/projet-de-renovation-croix-du-bonhomme.html. Stage 2 must end at Les Chapieux, or be split at Nant Borrant, Refuge de la Balme or Refuge des Prés. Walkers continuing over the Col des Fours should use Refuge des Mottets or Rifugio Elisabetta. https://dam.cicerone.co.uk/book/1228/Tour-of-Mont-Blanc-important-update.docx
- **Check seasonal openings.** The ideal season runs from mid-June to mid-September, and each refuge sets its own dates. Refuge des Mottets, for example, is open 10 June to 20 September 2027. https://www.montourdumontblanc.com/en/ ; https://www2.montourdumontblanc.com/en/refuges/refuge-des-mottets
- **Carry cash in euros and Swiss francs.** https://www2.montourdumontblanc.com/en/actualites/when-refuge-bookings-open-for-the-season
- **Respect current route changes.** The path between Prayon and Branche (Les Treutsebo, Swiss sector) was rerouted for rockfall safety from 11 July 2026, and camping and bivouacking are prohibited there. https://www.autourdumontblanc.com/en/
- **Check snow and conditions before going.** Snow on the high cols (Bonhomme, Seigne, Fenêtre d'Arpette) can persist into late June or July in some years. *(secondary)* https://www.10adventures.com/best-time-to-hike-the-tour-du-mont-blanc/. The official site carries a conditions banner. https://www2.montourdumontblanc.com/en/actualites/when-refuge-bookings-open-for-the-season

**Red flags**
- **The TMB as day hikes by car from one base**, for example staying in Chamonix and driving to trailheads. This is not a hut-to-hut trek.
- **A rental car as the main mode.**
- **Booking the Refuge de la Croix du Bonhomme** in July 2027.
- **Missing one of the three countries**, or no Italian section (Courmayeur, Val Ferret, Col de la Seigne) at all.
- **Stages over about 25 km or 1,500 m of ascent**, repeated or back to back; or walking that ends after about 20:00 on high ground.
- **High passes scheduled into the afternoon.** July afternoon thunderstorms are common, so walkers should start early. *unverified*
- **Wild camping or bivouacking** where it is banned, such as Les Treutsebo. https://www.autourdumontblanc.com/en/
- **Accommodation implied to be available on arrival** in mid-July.
- **Luggage shown as delivered to high refuges** such as Elisabetta, Bonatti or Flégère. *unverified*

**Timing facts (10–19 July 2027)**
- **Sun times** (computed; check at https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/france/chamonix):

  | Place and date | Sunrise | Sunset |
  |---|---|---|
  | Chamonix, 10 Jul | 05:52 | 21:23 |
  | Chamonix, 15 Jul | 05:56 | 21:20 |
  | Chamonix, 19 Jul | 06:00 | 21:17 |
  | Courmayeur, 15 Jul | 05:57 | 21:19 |
  | Champex-Lac, 15 Jul | 05:55 | 21:19 |

  These are horizon times. Valley floors lose direct sun earlier.
- **Crowds.** This is peak season. Demand is highest in July and August. https://www2.montourdumontblanc.com/en/actualites/booking-a-refuge-the-complete-guide. 14 July is the French national holiday. *unverified*
- **Weather.** Warm valleys of about 20–25 °C and cold cols, with regular afternoon thunderstorms in July. *(secondary)* https://www.macsadventure.com/us/blog/weather-conditions-tour-du-mont-blanc/. Lingering snow patches are possible on the north faces of the cols. *(secondary)* https://www.10adventures.com/best-time-to-hike-the-tour-du-mont-blanc/
- **Transport seasons.** The Val Ferret bus and the TMR Ferret–Champex bus run through July (see above).

**Reasonable daily limits**
- **Walking**: about 12–20 km a day, 5–8 hours including stops, and about 700–1,300 m of ascent. That is the range of the standard stages above.
- **Transfers** (buses, lifts, the airport run): typically under 1 hour a day, with at most about 2 hours on arrival or departure day from Geneva.
