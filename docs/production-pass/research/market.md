# Market scan: AI itinerary planners (as of 2026-10-24)

Scope: Wanderlog, Mindtrip, Layla, TripIt, Roadtrippers, Google (AI Mode Canvas / Gemini / Ask Maps), Stippl, Polarsteps, Airial, with a note on Tripadvisor. Most detail comes from third-party review or competitor sites, so claims are marked **[V]** when backed by first-party pages, app-store listings or user reviews, and **[U]** when they come from a single secondary or vendor-blog source.

## Product by product

### Wanderlog
- **Core and workflow:** A manual-first planner. The traveller searches and adds each stop to a combined itinerary-and-map view, and booking emails can be imported. Pro costs $39.99/yr and adds offline access, route optimisation, Google Maps export, Gmail auto-scan, and unlimited AI ([endlesstravelplans](https://www.endlesstravelplans.com/guides/planning-tools/wanderlog-review), checked July 2026) **[V-ish]**.
- **AI:** The free tier is capped at about 5 messages ([monkeytravel](https://monkeytravel.app/blog/wanderlog-alternative-2026)) **[U]**. The AI suggests places but does not build the plan.
- **Collaboration:** Live co-editing, view or edit permissions, and expense splitting. There is no voting and no group chat ([pilotplans](https://www.pilotplans.com/compare/wanderlog-alternative-feature-comparison), a competitor page) **[U]**.
- **Complaints:** Trustpilot rates it 1.9/5 from 51 reviews ([trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/wanderlog.com)) **[V]**:
  - a forced trial and surprise charges;
  - a wedding venue placed in the wrong city;
  - museums listed as free that cost €10;
  - forwarded booking emails that never reach the trip (Jul 2026);
  - duplicated items, and slowness on large trips.
  - An older review cites a 3 h drive estimate for a 40–50 min drive.
- **Takeaway:** Strong at organising a trip the traveller builds; weak on data correctness; the useful in-trip tools are paywalled.

### Mindtrip
- **Core:** Chat produces an editable plan with photos, reviews and a map ([mindtrip about](https://mindtrip.ai/about)) **[V]**. "Start Anywhere" builds a trip from a screenshot, TikTok, YouTube video or PDF ([TechCrunch, Jul 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/31/travel-startup-mindtrips-new-feature-lets-you-build-an-itinerary-from-a-screenshot-youtube-or-tiktok-video)) **[V]**.
- **Group:** Group Chat launched in Sep 2024. The bot "combines each traveller's preferences into one itinerary" ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/mindtrip-secures-12m-funding-launches-group-chat)) **[V launch; merge quality U]**.
- **Booking:** Partners include Priceline, Viator and Tripadvisor. Agentic flight booking on Sabre and PayPal (May 2026) and conversational hotel search with price-drop alerts (Jul 2026) appear in one directory only ([stork.ai](https://www.stork.ai/en/mindtrip)) **[U]**. The base tier is free and revenue comes from affiliate commissions.
- **Complaints:**
  - The itinerary "randomly refreshes and messes up the order", losing hours of work ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/mindtrip)) **[V]**.
  - Suggestions lean touristy, including a Barcelona park the city had removed from Maps directions because of crowds ([TravelAwaits](https://www.travelawaits.com/3005771/inside-new-travel-ai-mindtrip/)) **[V]**.
  - A trip can have only one destination, so cruises are not supported (App Store) **[V]**.
- **Takeaway:** The best inspiration-to-plan funnel in the category, but the plan is model output with no visible verification.

### Layla (layla.ai)
- **Core:** Chat plus templates the traveller can "remix", then a human Travel Advisor reviews the plan and books the package. Planning is free and revenue comes from bookings, with a €100/person/night recommended minimum and 24/7 WhatsApp care ([layla.ai](https://layla.ai)) **[V]**. Older reviews mention a $49/yr Premium tier for PDF export ([endlesstravelplans](https://www.endlesstravelplans.com/guides/planning-tools/layla-ai-review)) **[U, likely superseded]**.
- **Map:** None is mentioned on the homepage.
- **Complaints:** Charges after the trial ended and partial-refund disputes (Apr–May 2026). Trustpilot's own summary says the AI is "inconsistent, forgetful of earlier instructions… unable to produce a usable final plan." Another reviewer spent 90 minutes without getting a usable three-day plan ([trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/layla.ai?page=2)) **[V]**.
- **Takeaway:** Has moved from an AI planner to an AI-fronted agency. The human in the loop is the reliability layer.

### TripIt
- **Core:** Execution, not planning. Forwarding confirmations to plans@tripit.com builds the itinerary. It has offline reservation details and calendar sync, and is rated 4.8 from about 312K ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tripit-travel-planner/id311035142)) **[V]**.
- **Pro ($48.99/yr):** Flight alerts, alternate-flight suggestions, Risk Alerts (strikes, severe weather), Fare and Seat Tracker, visa and health "Travel Guidance". "Import from Photo/PDF" uses Apple Intelligence. There is no generative planning **[V]**.
- **Complaints:** Weak editing (no duplicate, no drag), lost notes when trips merge, unclear handling of multiple passengers, and hesitancy to grant email access **[V]**.
- **Takeaway:** The benchmark for disruption alerts and email import. It plans nothing.

### Roadtrippers
- **Core:** Road trips in the US and Canada. Map-first stop discovery, campground data, and overlays for public land, cell coverage and wildfire smoke, plus CarPlay. "Autopilot" is an AI wizard drawing on 38M trips ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/roadtrippers-trip-planner/id944060491)) **[V]**.
- **Pricing:** Annual Basic $35.99, Pro $49.99, Premium $59.99. The free tier allows 3 stops and Premium 150 **[V]**.
- **Complaints:** The free stop limit was cut. The planned route is discarded when navigation starts. Traffic closures are missed. Changing dates is painful. Dated stops land on separate days. Gas estimates are wrong **[V]**.
- **Takeaway:** Corridor discovery is strong; day structure and replanning are weak.

### Google (AI Mode Canvas, Gemini, Ask Maps)
- **Gemini:** Pulls Maps, Flights and Hotels data. "Personal Intelligence" reads Gmail, Photos, Search and YouTube history. "Gemini Spark" watches Gmail and builds a Google Doc itinerary as confirmations arrive. Viator bookings work in-chat ([Google blog, Aug 6 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/how-gemini-plans-trips/)) **[V]**.
- **AI Mode Canvas:** A side-panel editable itinerary, US-only. Other reported features **[U, single secondary source]**:
  - flight tracking across 180+ countries;
  - hotel help limited to 10 partners;
  - no checkout inside AI Mode;
  - Ask Maps (Mar 2026), mobile only, US and India ([tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/google-ai-travel-tools-trip-planning-2026/)).
- **Takeaway:** The largest threat. Google has free distribution, real place data, inbox access, and memory across products. What it does not show is constraint verification or a feasibility verdict. Its output is a Doc or a chat, not a checked plan.

### Stippl
- **Core:** An all-in-one app covering AI itinerary, budget allocation and splitting, packing, expenses and journaling. Pro is €24.99/yr and includes offline. There is comment-based collaboration and no voting. Booking links out to Booking.com and Airbnb ([Stippl blog](https://www.stippl.io/blog/stippl-vs-wanderlog), a vendor source) **[U]**. Stippl's own blog admits it is "still catching up on reliability."
- **Ratings:** About 3.7★, with complaints about stability and affiliate creep ([pilotplans](https://www.pilotplans.com/compare/stippl-alternative-feature-comparison)) **[U]**.

### Polarsteps
- **Core:** A GPS journal that tracks offline, with more than 20M users. It added an AI planner with destination snapshots that explain "why this fits your travel style" (App Store) **[V]**. Web planning, flight booking and a paid Plus tier (Aug 2026) are reported by an aggregator **[U]**.
- **Notable:** Its own guide tells users *not* to trust AI journey times and to cross-check with Google Maps or Rome2Rio ([Polarsteps](https://www.polarsteps.com/stories/how-to-use-ai-for-travel-planning)) **[V]**.

### Airial
- **Core:** Founded by ex-Google and ex-Meta engineers. It claims multi-leg itineraries with flights, hotels, ground transport and activities, plus import from TikTok and Instagram ([BusinessWire, Dec 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241205056478/en/Airial-Travel-Public-Beta-Takes-Off-Effortless-AI-Powered-Travel-Planning-is-Here)). The homepage now pitches a B2B "Airial for Business" ([airial.travel](https://www.airial.travel/)). There are no independent reviews **[U]**.

### Tripadvisor
- **Core:** An OpenAI-based Trips generator grounded in reviews, launched in 2023 ([Tripadvisor](https://tripadvisor.mediaroom.com/Tripadvisor-launches-AI-powered-travel-planning-product?asPDF=1)). No 2026 update was found **[U on current state]**.

### Category evidence on accuracy
In SEO Travel's ChatGPT study (May 2024), 90% of itineraries had at least one error:
- 52% scheduled visits outside opening hours;
- about 25% included closed venues;
- about 25% had poor routing ([travelnews.co.za](https://www.travelnews.co.za/article/ai-generated-itineraries-lead-travellers-astray?page=83)).

Neither ChatGPT nor any competitor publishes how often a whole plan is feasible.

## Patterns
1. **The money is in booking, not planning.** Mindtrip, Layla, Google, Stippl and Polarsteps all route to bookings. Planning quality is a loss leader, and nobody is accountable for whether the plan works.
2. **Plans are unverified model output.** User complaints cluster on wrong locations, wrong prices, closed or overcrowded places, and bad drive times. Even Polarsteps tells users to cross-check its times.
3. **Editing destroys work.** Mindtrip reorders by itself, Layla forgets instructions, Roadtrippers drops the route when navigation starts, and TripIt merges lose notes. No product offers safe partial replanning.
4. **Billing distrust is a top-three complaint** for Wanderlog, Layla and Roadtrippers (forced trials, auto-renewals).
5. **Execution and planning are split across products.** TripIt has alerts but no plan; the planners have no disruption handling. Google is the only one closing the gap, through Gmail.

## Opportunity assessment

| Opportunity | Competitor state | Sidequest position | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verified constraints (hours, drive times, feasibility verdict) | No product shows one. This is the main complaint category | Built: reconcile, feasibility report, quality compiler, "unknown ≠ false" | **Very high** |
| Source-confidence transparency | None. Polarsteps admits the problem | Built: provenance, `basis`, measured vs estimated legs, placement ladder | **High**, if made legible |
| Partial replanning | Broken everywhere (reorders, forgets) | Built: patch-not-rewrite, local day edits, Undo, delta before Apply | **Very high** |
| "What to skip" decision support | Absent. Mindtrip skews touristy | Planner records a reason for every drop; board has "things to skip" | **High** |
| Import (Google Maps lists, emails, screenshots, existing plans) | TripIt, Google and Wanderlog do email; Mindtrip does social | Booking-confirmation import exists. Lists and social links do not | **High**, table stakes for adoption |
| Weather/disruption-aware adjustment | TripIt does alerts only; nobody re-plans | Recheck exists (detect → propose → accept) | **Medium–high** |
| Group preference collaboration | Mindtrip has chat; Wanderlog co-edits; nobody votes or merges transparently | Party members with per-person hard rules exist; no shared voting | **Medium–high** |
| Persistent traveller memory | Google wins by default (Personal Intelligence) | `preference_evidence` learns taste only | **Medium**. Cannot out-memory Google |
| Offline access | Paywalled (Wanderlog, Stippl); TripIt shows reservations offline | Unverified for Sidequest | **Medium**, expected for in-trip use |
| Budget optimisation | Tracking and splitting only (Stippl, Wanderlog) | Frequency logic ("one splurge"), budget bundles | **Medium**. Real optimisation needs live prices, which Sidequest refuses to show |

## Top 6 differentiators to prioritise (ranked)

1. **A trip that is checked before it is shown.** Every stop is placed, every leg is measured or estimated with its basis labelled, hours are checked, and there is one honest Ready / Cautions / Not ready verdict. This answers the category's main complaint, and Google does not show it. Make the verdict the hero of the itinerary rather than an audit tab.
2. **Edit without losing the plan.** Surgical replanning: "swap day 3", "rain on Thursday", "drop the hike". Sidequest shows a diff, changes only what was asked, and offers exact Undo. This is the most-cited failure at Mindtrip, Layla and Roadtrippers, and Sidequest's patch architecture already does it. Expose it as one-tap actions.
3. **"Here's what we left out and why."** Present skip and drop reasoning as a first-class decision view covering low-fit famous places, too-far detours and tourist traps, with "add it back" showing the cost in minutes. Nobody does opinionated pruning, and it is what a human advisor sells.
4. **Start from what you already have.** Import a Google Maps saved list, a shared link, booking emails or PDFs, or a pasted rough plan, and feed the items onto the Discovery Board as traveller-status candidates. Then run Mode 3, critique, over it. This is the adoption wedge against Google's inbox advantage; without it, Sidequest asks users to start over.
5. **Visible confidence, not false precision.** Show plainly on each card and leg: "measured drive · Valhalla", "estimated · no route data", "hours unverified". It turns the "unknown ≠ false" doctrine into trust the traveller can see, and directly counters the "AI nonsense" reviews. Pair it with no forced trial and no affiliate-ranked results, given the billing complaints.
6. **Group decisions with hard rules.** Each member votes on board cards, and each member's hard constraints (diet, mobility, effort) are enforced, not averaged. The plan says whose pick each stop is. Mindtrip's group chat merges preferences opaquely; nobody shows the trade-offs.

**Second tier:** offline trip packet (needed for Today mode in the field, but not a differentiator), weather-triggered day swaps built on the existing recheck, and memory. Sidequest should not try to compete with Google on memory; let the traveller carry a profile across trips instead.

**Avoid:** chasing booking or affiliate parity. The incumbents already own it, and it undermines the neutrality that makes differentiators 1, 3 and 5 believable.
